# How Much Does a Website Cost in 2026? Honest Pricing Breakdown

It's the first question every business owner asks. And the answer they usually get — "it depends" — is technically true but completely unhelpful.

So here's the honest version. We're going to break down what websites actually cost in 2026, what you get at every price point, what's worth paying for, and what's a waste of money. No vague ranges. No upsell pitch. Just real numbers based on what we see in the market every day.

## Tier 1: DIY Website Builders — $0–$500/Year

**Platforms:** Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy Builder, Google Sites
**What you get:** A drag-and-drop website you build yourself using pre-made templates

This is the option your brother-in-law recommends. "Just use Wix, it's free." And for some situations, it's fine. If you need a simple online presence — a few pages, your phone number, a contact form — a DIY builder gets you there for under $300/year.

### What's Included

- Pre-designed templates (you customize colors, text, images)
- Basic contact form
- Mobile-responsive layout (built into the template)
- Hosting included in your monthly fee
- SSL certificate (HTTPS)
- Basic SEO fields (title tags, meta descriptions)

### What's Missing

- Custom design — your site looks like thousands of others using the same template
- Speed optimization — builders load heavy scripts that slow your site down
- Advanced SEO — limited control over page structure, schema markup, and technical settings
- Conversion optimization — templates aren't designed to generate leads for your specific business
- Scalability — adding complex features (booking systems, member portals, custom calculators) is limited or impossible
- Ownership — you don't own the code. Leave the platform and you start over from scratch

### Who This Is Actually For

- Freelancers who just need a portfolio page
- Businesses that get 100% of their leads from word of mouth and just need a digital business card
- Side projects and personal sites
- Businesses testing a new idea before investing

### Who Should Skip This

Anyone who relies on their website to generate leads, sales, or phone calls. A DIY site might save you $5,000 upfront, but if it costs you 10 leads per month for two years, you didn't save anything — you lost $50,000+ in revenue you never saw.

## Tier 2: Template Website — $1,500–$5,000

**Built by:** Freelance designer or small agency
**Platforms:** WordPress + premium theme, Elementor, Divi
**What you get:** A professionally set up website using a pre-built theme customized to your brand

This is where most new small businesses start — and it's a reasonable choice if your budget is tight and your needs are straightforward. A designer takes a premium WordPress theme, adds your logo, colors, content, and images, and delivers a clean site in 2–4 weeks.

### What's Included

- Premium WordPress theme (Astarter, Elementor template, etc.)
- 5–10 pages (Home, About, Services, Contact, etc.)
- Your branding applied (logo, colors, fonts)
- Mobile-responsive design
- Contact form setup
- Basic SEO setup (Yoast or Rank Math, title tags, meta descriptions)
- Google Analytics installation
- 1–2 rounds of revisions
- Hosting setup (you pay hosting separately — $10–$50/month)

### What's Missing

- Custom design — it's a theme with your colors on it, not a design built for your business goals
- Conversion strategy — no user research, no wireframing, no A/B testing
- Content strategy — you usually have to provide your own copy (or pay extra)
- Advanced functionality — custom calculators, booking systems, client portals require custom development
- Ongoing support — most freelancers deliver the site and move on. Updates, security, and fixes are on you.
- Page speed optimization — theme bloat can slow load times

### The Hidden Cost

Cheap themes come with bloated code, unnecessary plugins, and security vulnerabilities. A $59 theme with 15 bundled plugins might work fine on day one — but six months later, when three plugins haven't been updated and your site loads in 6 seconds, you'll pay someone to fix it or start over.

### Who This Is Actually For

- New businesses with limited budget and simple needs
- Companies that need a professional online presence but aren't ready for a major investment
- Businesses with straightforward services (plumber, landscaper, consultant)

## Tier 3: Custom Small Business Website — $5,000–$15,000

**Built by:** Web design agency or experienced studio
**Platforms:** WordPress + custom theme, Webflow, or custom-coded
**What you get:** A website designed from scratch for your specific business, audience, and goals

This is where the ROI starts. A custom website isn't just prettier — it's built with strategy. The design decisions are based on how your customers think, what they search for, and what makes them pick up the phone.

### What's Included

- Custom design — wireframes, mockups, and a layout built specifically for your business
- 8–20 pages (including service pages, city/location pages, about, contact, FAQ)
- Professional copywriting (or copy editing of your content)
- Mobile-first responsive design
- SEO foundation — proper heading hierarchy, meta tags, schema markup, XML sitemap, fast load times
- Google Analytics and conversion tracking setup
- Contact forms, click-to-call, and lead capture optimized for conversions
- Speed optimization — image compression, caching, minimal plugin usage
- 2–3 rounds of revisions
- 30–90 days of post-launch support
- CMS training so you can update content yourself

### What Makes This Worth the Money

**Strategy before design.** Before a single pixel is pushed, a good agency asks: Who are your customers? What do they search for? What makes them choose you over competitors? What does a lead look like? The answers shape every design decision.

**Pages built to rank.** Each service page and city page is structured for SEO — proper H1s, internal linking, keyword targeting, schema markup. A template site gives you 5 generic pages. A custom site gives you 15–20 pages, each one targeting a specific keyword and designed to attract organic traffic.

**Conversion-focused design.** Strategic CTA placement, clear value propositions above the fold, trust signals (reviews, certifications, project photos), and a user flow that guides visitors from landing to lead form. The difference between a 1% and 5% conversion rate on a page getting 1,000 visitors/month is 40 extra leads per month — worth far more than the cost difference between a template and custom site.

### Who This Is For

- Established businesses that rely on their website for leads
- Service businesses (contractors, medical practices, law firms, agencies)
- Companies ready to invest in marketing and growth
- Businesses that want to rank on Google for their core services

## Tier 4: Advanced Custom Website — $15,000–$40,000

**Built by:** Established agency with development team
**Platforms:** WordPress + custom development, Webflow, headless CMS, Next.js
**What you get:** Everything in Tier 3 plus advanced functionality, integrations, and complex architecture

### What's Included (Beyond Tier 3)

- Complex site architecture (50+ pages, multi-location, multiple service lines)
- Custom functionality — calculators, configurators, client portals, booking systems
- CRM integration (HubSpot, Salesforce, etc.)
- Multi-location pages with location-specific content
- Advanced animations and interactions
- Custom blog templates with category filtering
- API integrations (payment processing, inventory, scheduling tools)
- Accessibility compliance (WCAG 2.1)
- Performance optimization for Core Web Vitals
- 6–12 months of ongoing support and maintenance

### Who This Is For

- Multi-location businesses
- Companies with complex service offerings or product lines
- Businesses that need custom tools (cost estimators, appointment scheduling, quote builders)
- Organizations that require integrations with existing business systems

## Tier 5: Ecommerce Website — $5,000–$50,000+

**Platforms:** Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, custom builds
**What you get:** An online store that lets customers browse, select, and purchase products

The range here is massive because ecommerce complexity varies wildly. A 20-product Shopify store is a completely different project than a 5,000-SKU custom WooCommerce build with inventory management, subscription billing, and wholesale pricing tiers.

### Ecommerce Pricing by Complexity

| Store Type | Price Range | Platform |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| Simple store (under 50 products) | $5,000–$10,000 | Shopify, Squarespace |
| Medium store (50–500 products) | $10,000–$25,000 | Shopify Plus, WooCommerce |
| Complex store (500+ products, custom features) | $25,000–$50,000+ | WooCommerce, custom build |

### Key Ecommerce Costs People Forget

- **Product photography** — $25–$100 per product for professional photos. Multiply that by your catalog.
- **Payment processing fees** — 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction (Stripe/PayPal standard)
- **Monthly platform fees** — Shopify Basic is $39/month. Shopify Plus starts at $2,300/month.
- **Shipping integration** — connecting to UPS, USPS, FedEx for real-time rates
- **Inventory management** — syncing online inventory with physical inventory
- **Ongoing maintenance** — plugin updates, security patches, platform updates

## Tier 6: Web Applications — $25,000–$100,000+

**Platforms:** React, Next.js, Node.js, Python/Django, custom stacks
**What you get:** Custom software built for your business — not a website with pages, but a tool that does something

This includes SaaS products, customer portals, dashboards, internal business tools, and platforms. If your project involves user accounts, data processing, complex logic, or real-time functionality, you're in web application territory.

We won't go deep here because if you need a web application, you already know it's a different conversation. Budget $50,000–$100,000+ for anything meaningful, and expect a 3–6+ month timeline.

## The Costs People Forget

The sticker price of a website is never the full cost. Here's what else you'll pay for:

### Ongoing Costs

| Item | Monthly Cost |
|------|-------------|
| Hosting | $10–$100/month (shared to managed) |
| Domain name | $12–$50/year |
| SSL certificate | Free (Let's Encrypt) to $200/year |
| Plugin/theme licenses | $100–$500/year |
| Security monitoring | $10–$30/month |
| Backups | $5–$20/month (or included with hosting) |
| Email hosting | $6–$12/user/month (Google Workspace) |

### Maintenance and Updates

A website isn't a set-it-and-forget-it asset. WordPress sites need plugin updates, security patches, PHP version updates, content refreshes, and performance monitoring. Budget $100–$500/month for a maintenance plan, or plan to spend 2–4 hours per month doing it yourself.

### Content Creation

Your website is a shell without content. If you don't have copy, photos, and videos ready, factor in:
- **Copywriting:** $500–$3,000 for full website copy
- **Photography:** $500–$2,000 for a professional shoot
- **Video:** $1,000–$5,000 for a brand video

## What Actually Determines the Price

Two websites can look identical but cost $3,000 and $15,000. Here's what drives the difference:

### 1. Custom Design vs. Template
A template is $1,500. A custom design with wireframes, mockups, and user research is $5,000+. You're paying for strategy, not pixels.

### 2. Number of Pages
A 5-page site takes a week. A 30-page site with service pages, city pages, and blog templates takes a month. More pages = more design, more content, more development time.

### 3. Functionality
A contact form is included. A custom quote calculator, booking system, or member portal is custom development — $2,000–$10,000+ depending on complexity.

### 4. Content
If you provide finished copy and professional photos, you save thousands. If the agency writes every word and sources every image, that's additional work.

### 5. Integrations
Connecting your website to a CRM, email marketing tool, payment processor, or scheduling software adds complexity and cost.

### 6. Who Builds It
A freelancer in the Philippines charges $500. A freelancer in Sacramento charges $3,000. An agency charges $8,000–$15,000. You're paying for experience, reliability, communication, and accountability — not just the code.

## How to Know What You Need

Ask yourself these questions:

**How do customers find you right now?**
If it's 100% word of mouth, a Tier 2 template site might be fine for now. If you need Google to send you leads, invest in Tier 3 minimum.

**What happens when someone visits your website?**
If you need them to call, fill out a form, book an appointment, or buy something — you need a site built for conversion, not just appearance.

**How many services and locations do you have?**
One service in one city = simple site. Five services across ten cities = you need 50+ pages with a proper SEO architecture. That's Tier 3 or 4.

**What's a customer worth to you?**
If your average job is $500, a $15,000 website needs to generate 30 leads to pay for itself. If your average job is $10,000, it only needs 2. The ROI math matters more than the sticker price.

**Are you running ads?**
If you're spending $3,000/month on Google Ads that send traffic to a slow, ugly website with no clear call to action — fix the website first. A better website makes every marketing dollar work harder.

## What Should You Spend?

Here's a simple framework:

**If your business generates under $100K/year:** $1,500–$5,000 (Tier 2)
**If your business generates $100K–$500K/year:** $5,000–$15,000 (Tier 3)
**If your business generates $500K–$2M/year:** $10,000–$25,000 (Tier 3–4)
**If your business generates $2M+/year:** $15,000–$40,000+ (Tier 4)

A website should cost roughly 2–5% of your annual revenue. Less than that and you're probably cutting corners. More than that and you're overbuilding for your current stage.
