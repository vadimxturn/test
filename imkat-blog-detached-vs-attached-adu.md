## H1: Detached vs. Attached ADU: Pros, Cons & Cost Comparison

If you're planning to build an ADU in California, the first major decision you'll face is whether to go detached or attached. Both add living space, rental income potential, and property value — but they differ in cost, construction timeline, privacy, and how they affect your existing home.

This guide breaks down the real differences between detached and attached ADUs so you can make the right call for your property, budget, and long-term goals.

---

## H2: What's the Difference Between a Detached and Attached ADU?

A **detached ADU** is a standalone structure built separately from your main house. It has its own foundation, walls, roof, and entrance. Think of it as a small independent home in your backyard.

An **attached ADU** shares at least one wall with your primary residence. It's built as an extension of your existing home — similar to a home addition, but with its own entrance, kitchen, and bathroom to qualify as a legal ADU under California law.

There's also a third option — a **garage conversion** — where you convert an existing attached or detached garage into living space. For this comparison, we're focusing on new-construction attached vs. detached units.

## H2: Cost Comparison: Detached vs. Attached ADU in California

Cost is usually the deciding factor. Here's what California homeowners should expect in 2026:

### H3: Detached ADU Cost

| ADU Size | Estimated Cost |
|----------|---------------|
| 400–500 sq ft (studio/1BR) | $150,000–$200,000 |
| 600–750 sq ft (2BR) | $200,000–$280,000 |
| 750–1,200 sq ft (3BR) | $280,000–$380,000 |

Detached ADUs cost more because they require:
- A new foundation (slab or raised)
- Full framing, roofing, and exterior finishes
- Separate utility connections (sewer, water, electrical)
- Potential trenching across your yard for utility lines

### H3: Attached ADU Cost

| ADU Size | Estimated Cost |
|----------|---------------|
| 400–500 sq ft (studio/1BR) | $120,000–$170,000 |
| 600–750 sq ft (2BR) | $170,000–$240,000 |
| 750–1,000 sq ft (3BR) | $240,000–$320,000 |

Attached ADUs are typically **15–25% cheaper** than detached because they share a wall (and sometimes a roof) with the main house. Utility connections are shorter and simpler since they tie into existing lines.

### H3: Where the Cost Savings Come From

| Cost Factor | Detached | Attached |
|-------------|----------|----------|
| Foundation | Full new slab | Partial — shares existing |
| Roofing | Full new roof | Extends existing roofline |
| Exterior walls | 4 walls | 3 walls (1 shared) |
| Utility trenching | 20–60 ft of trenching | Minimal — ties into house |
| Site prep | Grading, drainage | Less disruption |

## H2: Pros and Cons of a Detached ADU

### H3: Pros

- **Maximum privacy.** Separate structure means no shared walls, no sound transfer, and independent entrances. Ideal for tenants or aging parents who want their own space.
- **Higher rental income.** Tenants pay more for a standalone unit. A detached 1BR ADU in Sacramento rents for $1,800–$2,500/month compared to $1,400–$2,000 for a comparable attached unit.
- **Flexible placement.** Can be positioned anywhere on your lot — backyard, side yard, or replacing an old shed. Not limited by the footprint of your existing house.
- **No disruption to your main home.** Construction happens away from the house. No dust, noise, or workers inside your living space.
- **Better resale value.** Buyers see a detached ADU as a separate "guest house" or "rental unit" — it carries more perceived value.

### H3: Cons

- **Higher cost.** 15–25% more than attached due to full foundation, 4-wall framing, and utility trenching.
- **Longer construction timeline.** Typically 6–9 months vs. 4–7 months for attached.
- **Utility connection costs.** Running sewer, water, and electrical lines across your yard adds $8,000–$20,000 depending on distance.
- **Uses more yard space.** The structure plus required setbacks can eat into usable outdoor area.
- **May require more permitting.** Some cities have stricter setback and height rules for detached structures.

---

## H2: Pros and Cons of an Attached ADU

### H3: Pros

- **Lower cost.** Sharing a wall and roof with the main house saves on materials, foundation, and labor.
- **Faster build time.** 4–7 months from permit to completion vs. 6–9 months for detached.
- **Simpler utility connections.** Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC can tie directly into existing systems.
- **Preserves yard space.** Since it extends from the house, you keep more of your backyard.
- **Easier accessibility.** Connected to the main home — ideal for aging parents or family members who need proximity.

### H3: Cons

- **Less privacy.** Shared wall means potential sound transfer. Tenants may feel less independent.
- **Lower rental premium.** Renters generally pay less for an attached unit than a standalone home.
- **Construction disrupts your home.** Workers need access inside or along your house during the build.
- **Design constraints.** The ADU's layout is dictated by where it connects to your home. Less flexibility in floor plan.
- **Structural considerations.** Tying into an existing structure may require engineering assessments of the main house's foundation and framing.

## H2: Which Type of ADU Should You Build?

### H3: Build a Detached ADU If:

- You plan to **rent it out** and want maximum monthly income
- Your lot is **large enough** (5,000+ sq ft with room behind or beside the house)
- Privacy matters — for you, your tenant, or an aging parent who values independence
- You don't want **construction disrupting** your daily life inside the home
- You're building for **long-term property value** and future resale

### H3: Build an Attached ADU If:

- Your **budget is tighter** and you want to save 15–25% on construction
- Your **lot is smaller** and you can't fit a detached structure within setback requirements
- The ADU is for **family** — aging parents, adult children, or guests who want to be close
- You want a **faster timeline** to completion
- You plan to use it as a **home office or studio** connected to your living space

### H3: Consider a Garage Conversion If:

- You have an existing garage you don't use
- Budget is the primary constraint ($80,000–$150,000 range)
- You want the fastest path to a finished unit
- You're OK with the size limitations of the existing structure

## H2: ADU Regulations in California: Attached vs. Detached

California's ADU laws (AB 2221, SB 897, AB 976) treat attached and detached ADUs similarly, but there are a few differences:

- **Size limits.** Detached ADUs can be up to 1,200 sq ft. Attached ADUs are capped at 50% of the primary home's living area (or 1,200 sq ft, whichever is less) — though many cities now allow up to 1,200 sq ft regardless.
- **Setbacks.** Detached ADUs require a minimum 4-foot rear and side setback. Attached ADUs follow the setback rules of the primary structure.
- **Height.** Detached ADUs can go up to 16 feet (or 18 feet in some cases). Attached ADUs match the main house's allowed height.
- **Parking.** Neither type requires additional parking in most California cities since AB 68.

Check your specific city's rules — Sacramento, San Jose, and Contra Costa County each have their own local interpretations of state law.

## H2: Frequently Asked Questions

### H3: Is a detached or attached ADU cheaper to build?
Attached ADUs are typically 15–25% cheaper because they share a wall, roof, and utility connections with the main house. For a 2-bedroom unit, that can mean saving $30,000–$50,000.

### H3: Which type of ADU has higher rental income?
Detached ADUs command higher rent because tenants value the privacy and independence of a standalone unit. In the Sacramento metro, the premium is typically $200–$500/month more than a comparable attached unit.

### H3: Can I build both an attached and detached ADU on my property?
Yes. California law allows one ADU plus one JADU (Junior ADU) on a single-family lot. Some homeowners build a detached ADU in the backyard and convert part of their home into a JADU.

### H3: How long does it take to build each type?
Detached ADUs typically take 6–9 months from permit approval to move-in. Attached ADUs take 4–7 months. Permitting adds 2–6 months before construction begins, depending on your city.

### H3: Does a detached ADU add more property value than attached?
Generally yes. Appraisers and buyers view a detached ADU as a separate rentable unit, which increases the property's income potential. Attached ADUs add value too, but are often perceived more like a home addition.

### H3: Do I need separate utility meters for a detached ADU?
Not required by California state law, but some cities and utility companies may require separate meters for water or electricity. Your builder should clarify this during the permit phase.
